Monograph Note • Portfolio & Fund Mechanics

The Quiet Arithmetic of Compounding & OCF Fee Drag

Abstract: In most consumer purchases, paying 1% more is trivial. In long-horizon compounding, a 1% annual fee does not cost 1% of your wealth—it surrenders nearly a third of your final retirement pot. (Tap dotted terms in the text to inspect definitions.)

Human intuition evolved to understand linear relationships, not exponential ones. When an investor compares a low-cost global index tracker charging an Ongoing Charges Figure (OCF) of 0.15% against a traditional actively managed fund charging 1.15%, the 1.00% difference appears harmless on a quarterly statement.

Glossary — Ongoing Charges Figure (OCF): The standardized UK/UCITS annual percentage deducted directly from a fund's net asset value to cover management, administration, and custody costs. It excludes internal portfolio transaction costs.

Yet because fees are deducted from the entire capital base every single year, you lose not only the fee itself, but also all future real compounding that pound would have generated over the remaining decades. Use the interactive monograph model below to test this across time horizons.

Glossary — Real Compounding: Investment growth measured after deducting the erosive effect of inflation, reflecting true purchasing power in today's pounds (£).
